# Candidates — Angry Judge: The Short Rant Contest

Gate: one text, 1–400 characters, written to `/output/entry.txt`.
Judge criteria, in the judge's own order: (1) a funny rant about one
everyday annoyance, not an essay or a list; (2) actually funny — anger
that feels shared, not performed; (3) a specific, concrete annoyance;
(4) sharp, economical wording, no throat-clearing; (5) a fresh angle.
Winners-study overlay: one concrete scene beats any abstraction.

## The ten drafts

1. **Smoke detector.** "The smoke detector waits until 3 a.m. to
   announce its battery is dying. One chirp. I drag a chair over and
   climb up in my underwear, and it goes silent — silent all day,
   silent all evening, a model citizen — until my head touches the
   pillow. Then: chirp. It is not dying. It is hunting."
2. **Self-checkout.** "The self-checkout has accused me of stealing a
   bag. A bag I paid five cents for, in front of a camera, a scale,
   and a teenager whose entire job is to decide if I look like a man
   who steals bags. I came in for milk. I am leaving as a suspect."
3. **Fitted sheet.** "Every night at 3 a.m. the corner of my fitted
   sheet gives up. Not the whole sheet — one corner, the far one, so
   I wake up lying on bare mattress like a raccoon in a dumpster
   while the sheet bunches under my ribs. I tuck it. It knows. It
   waits. It has never once kept a promise."
4. **Group chat.** "Fourteen people are in the group chat to pick a
   dinner date. There are 212 messages. There are memes, a baby
   photo, and someone asking what the chat is for. There is no date.
   There will never be a date. We will all die in this chat, still
   typing 'any weekend works for me!'"
5. **Duvet cover.** "Putting on a duvet cover is wrestling a ghost
   that has already won. You climb inside it like a fool, grab two
   corners, and emerge four minutes later sweating, with the duvet in
   a ball at the bottom and one corner mocking you from inside the
   fabric. Nobody has ever done this gracefully. Nobody."
6. **Empty milk carton.** "Someone in this house drinks the last of
   the milk and puts the empty carton back in the fridge. Not the
   bin — the fridge, upright, cap on, so the next person lifts it
   with hope in their heart and feels it weigh nothing. That is not
   laziness. That is stagecraft. That is a magician's trick played
   on family."
7. **Shower dial.** "My shower has two temperatures: arctic
   expedition and soup. The dial moves in millimetres — one hair to
   the left and I am being pressure-washed in the Antarctic, one hair
   to the right and I am an ingredient. Somewhere between them is
   'warm', a place no human has ever been."
8. **USB plug.** "A USB plug has two sides and I need three tries.
   First try: no. Flip it: no. Flip it back to the way that just
   failed: in. Physicists have names for particles that behave like
   this. I have names for it too, but my grandmother reads these."
9. **Lift button.** "The lift button is already lit. It was lit when
   you arrived. Pressing it again does not summon a second, angrier
   lift. And yet every day a grown adult stabs it four times like the
   building responds to enthusiasm. The doors open at exactly the
   same speed, judging all of us."
10. **Moved snooze button.** "Every update moves one button on my
    phone. Last night it moved the alarm's snooze, so at 6 a.m.,
    half asleep, I pressed where snooze has lived for six years and
    turned the alarm OFF. I woke up at nine, unemployed-adjacent,
    betrayed by a button that moved in the night like furniture in a
    poltergeist film."

## The duel

- All ten pass criterion 1 (one annoyance each, no lists).
- **8 (USB)** falls first on criterion 5: the three-flip joke is a
  decade old; the judge has heard it a hundred times.
- **7 (shower)** and **9 (lift)** are concrete but the beats are
  familiar; they smirk more than they laugh (criterion 2).
- **4 (group chat)** is fresh and modern but the anger is wry, not
  bodily; **5 (duvet)** is bodily and funny but sprawls (criterion 4).
- **3 (fitted sheet)** has the scene and the body but a softer close.
- **6 (milk carton)** builds beautifully — "stagecraft" is a real
  laugh — but its anger is at a person, slightly performed.
- Final: **2 (self-checkout)** vs **1 (smoke detector)**. The
  self-checkout is the most economical text here and "leaving as a
  suspect" lands hard. The smoke detector wins on criteria 3 and 5:
  it is the most specific scene in the set (3 a.m., a chair, standing
  in underwear, the pillow) and its angle — the chirp that performs
  innocence all day and hunts at night — is one the judge has not
  heard. Its anger is the most purely shared: everyone has stood on
  that chair.

**Winner: 1, the smoke detector.**
